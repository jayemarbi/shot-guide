# Shot Guide

An iPhone app for filming the jodyseescoolstuff shot list. Start a trip at a place, pick its type (Mountain and hiking, Waterfalls and rainforest, Beach and coast, City, Road trip, Snow or General), then pick any of the 29 shots (extras are marked) and the camera opens with that shot's settings. The trip page lists them in the order you'll film them through the day: on the way (HOME · BEFORE at home or the gate, then the trip there), arriving (WHIP · IN, POV, HYPERLAPSE and FOLLOW as you walk in), first look (REACTION and REVEAL), at the spot (you in the view, easiest first, the ones worth taking your time over last: WIDE, SLOMO, TALK, WALKAWAY, TELE, MATCH CUT, MOMENT, ORBIT, CLONE and DANCE, then SILHOUETTE at sunset), B-roll (the place without you, to grab all day: B-ROLL, DETAIL, REFLECTION, WILDLIFE, FOOD, SOUNDS, AMBIENT and TIMELAPSE) and leaving (WHIP · OUT, VOICEOVER, STAY when you first walk into where you're sleeping, then HOME · AFTER in bed back home), with a line about when golden hour and sunset are. The type tunes which shots count, how many takes each needs and the tips; snow days film a little brighter. Days are added as you go: each day has its own shot list and counts, and can move to a new place with its own type.

**Creative coverage (1.19).** The aim is variety per minute of filming, not a pile of files. Shots that need several takes give each one a creative role, picked on the trip page or the camera and saved with the clip:

- WIDE: Establishing, Me in the view and Another angle, plus an optional upright Vertical hero.
- B-ROLL: Atmosphere, People, Movement, Local character, New lens or angle and Transition, with Nature and Into the light to swap in where a place has no people, say.
- DETAIL: Texture, Object, Hands, Foreground and Only here, with Nature to swap in.
- POV: Approach and Moving through.
- REVEAL: any two of its versions.
- WALKAWAY: Into the view, and New spot or action.
- MOMENT: With the place, and Together.

On the trip page each of these shots lists its roles as small named chips: green once a take counts (a filled tick once it's confirmed), plain while it's still to film, outlined for optional roles and swap-ins, and with an orange warning when the last take didn't count. Tap a chip to open the camera on that role, so you can film the roles in whatever order the places come.

Each role has its own framing, camera settings, steps and length, and remembers your timing on its own. A role you never changed starts from what you'd set for the whole shot before roles. One take fills one role, so repeats are extras: welcome and catalogued, never an obligation.

A take that passed its take check (or has none) and wasn't stopped short counts for now. It's confirmed once you mark it Usable on its review screen, or star a passing take as the best one. Use anyway counts a take despite its check, and Not usable stops one counting. None of this is required.

The trip page shows three levels built from the same takes: a light day (10 clips on a general day), everyday (22) and a full day (30, adding REACTION, TELE, TALK, SLOMO, MATCH CUT and SILHOUETTE). It also shows what each level still needs, role by role, and a Next button for the next useful role. Specialist shots stay extras, and trip types adjust the counts. Falls ask for 5 DETAIL and 2 AMBIENT, cities for 2 FOOD and a FOLLOW, and road trips for 4 ON THE WAY.

Scene coverage groups each day's clips into scenes, one per place, and shows what each scene's story still needs:

- Establish (WIDE, REVEAL)
- Approach (POV, WALKAWAY)
- Interact (DETAIL, MOMENT with the place)
- Experience (MOMENT together, WIDE with you in it)
- Atmosphere (AMBIENT, B-ROLL)

The app suggests a new scene more than 1 km from the last clip, or 45 minutes after it when there's no location. You can start a new scene, keep the next clip in the last one, merge a scene with the one before, split one at a clip, or move a clip. Scenes never change the day's counts.

**Two places in a day (1.21).** On a hike with two waterfalls, tap New place when you reach the second, or Name it when the app spots that you've moved on. Its clips get the name, so files read like `Springbrook_2026-11-08_WIDE_04_Twin-Falls.mov`, and a clip further along at the same place picks the name up too. Name or rename any place from its scene's menu: clips without a name, or with its old name, are renamed, files included, and clips you named yourself keep theirs. Once a day has been at more than one place, the shots that make each place its own (REACTION, REVEAL, WIDE, TELE, WALKAWAY, MOMENT, SLOMO, MATCH CUT and AMBIENT, since 1.22) show what you've filmed at the place you're at, under its name: their role chips, or one To film here / Filmed here chip. So the second falls gets its own set. B-ROLL, DETAIL, POV and the rest are gathered for the day as a whole. The counts, levels and Next still cover the whole day, so the second set are extras.

**One take, several shots (1.24).** Once you press record, the phone can't change anything: its spot, height, lens, frame rate and exposure stay put. So the shots that only differ in what you do can share one take, and you're not walking back to the phone between them. ONE TAKE, at the top of At the spot, films these from one tripod set-up (hip height, 0.5x, 60 fps, focus, exposure and white balance locked):

- Empty frame (WIDE · Establishing, and CLONE's clean background), 0:30
- Walk in (WALKAWAY), 0:30
- In the view (WIDE · Me in the view), 1:00
- Your moment (MOMENT), 1:00
- Your MATCH CUT move, 0:30: with the light on, walk slowly towards the phone until it goes off, then 5 flashes and the move
- Clones (CLONE), 2:30, walking out for the last 30 seconds

Tap a part to leave it out, like MATCH CUT and CLONE when you'd rather film them elsewhere along the trail, and set how long each runs: both are remembered. Your remote moves you on to the next part whenever you're ready, so long parts never hold you up. After the last part a fast flicker says walk back, and it keeps rolling to film you coming back until you stop it. It's saved as one video. Each part counts as a take of its shot, for the role it still needs where you are (one part, one role), and the catalogue lists each part with where it starts and ends in the file. A second ONE TAKE from a new spot fills the second roles (WIDE · Another angle, WALKAWAY · New spot or action, MOMENT · Together). The review lists the parts with a Play button and a verdict for each: mark one not usable if you skipped it, and its role stays open. Next suggests ONE TAKE when two or more of its shots are still needed. TELE (the 5x), SLOMO (120 fps), SILHOUETTE (darkened), DANCE (the song), TALK (front camera, upright), TIMELAPSE and WIDE's vertical hero can't join, as the phone has to change for them.

**Signals while you're in the shot (1.24).** Every signal is 5 flashes, one a second, leading into what's next, the same as the countdown: when the fifth is done, the next step starts. A fast flicker means the take has stopped (or ONE TAKE's parts are done): walk back. With AirPods or headphones in, a voice says what's next as the flashes start, and the countdown too. The screen dims when you press record on a tripod shot you're in and comes back when the take ends, as nobody's looking at it and a bright screen heats the phone. The shots you're in have longer default lengths too (WIDE with you in it, WALKAWAY, MOMENT and TELE), as walking back costs more than trimming.

**MATCH CUT moves (1.24).** One move, done once at every place, cut together on the same moment of the move so it looks like one move while the places change. Pick the move on the camera screen: Run across, Leap, Arms out, Spin, Fall back, Snap (upright) or Ridge walk (5x). Each has its own settings, and the one you did last is picked next time. Each shows how many places you've filmed it at, across every trip. Press record and stand where you'll do it, facing the phone: the light stays on while the camera finds you and zooms until you're the same size as in your first take of that move, then goes off, and 5 flashes count you in. Your first take of a move sets the size; New size starts again. Your remote starts it at the size you are if it can't find you.

**Skip (1.24).** Skip on the Next row brings the next shot forward. The skipped one is back after your next take, so you can keep skipping it until you're somewhere it suits.

**All shots (1.20).** A row under the golden hour line on the trip page opens every shot in the order of the day, grouped like the trip page with where you'll be for each part. Each shot has its own page: what it's for and what you'd expect to see in it (the trail through the forest, the ridge with the range beside you, the falls, the bay, the street), how to hold the phone and what it goes on, how many a day needs, each role or version with its camera settings, set-up and where you stand, and the tips. It follows the day's trip type, and it only shows information: nothing on the trip changes.

Every shot says which way to hold the phone and what it goes on, on the trip page and the camera screen, like "Landscape · Tripod". The big views (WIDE, TELE, WALKAWAY, MOMENT, SILHOUETTE, TIMELAPSE, REFLECTION, MATCH CUT, ON THE WAY, WILDLIFE, HOME and B-ROLL) are filmed in landscape, for the letterboxed, cinematic edits; you talking or up close (TALK, REACTION, POV, FOOD, FOLLOW and WHIP) is filmed in portrait, for full-screen reels; the rest can go either way. The still shots go on the tripod (and WIDE and SLOMO when you're in them), the smooth moving ones on the gimbal (FOLLOW, POV, ORBIT, HYPERLAPSE, STAY, TALK's walking version and REVEAL's walk and tilt up), and the rest are handheld. The camera says when the phone is held the other way, and records the way it's held, like the Camera app, so landscape clips come out wide and the right way up (the front camera always records in portrait). The words on screen, the level line and drag-to-brighten all turn with the phone.

The camera has pinch to zoom (up to 20x; it resets after each take), a level line and a grid that can be switched off. Every shot has a countdown (off up to 10 minutes) and either stops by itself after a length you choose or keeps rolling until you stop it; the app remembers your choices for each shot, and for each version of one. Every shot has a How it works card: the setup (including which way to hold the phone and what it goes on), where you are in the frame for the shots you're in, a walkthrough with rough times, and optional tips. It opens before every take of a shot you're in and the first time you open the others, and the How it works button brings it back any time. Phone-in-hand shots show a small prompt along the top of the frame while you record (1.23): the clock, what to do now in a few words, a bar for the step and what's next and when, turned to face you whichever way you hold the phone. Since 1.24 it shows on any shot where you can see the screen while you film: the front-camera shots, REACTION once it's turned round to the view, and ONE TAKE while you're behind the phone. When you change a shot's length, its walkthrough and prompts stretch the main action, while short steps like getting going and easing to a stop keep their length (the shots you're in too, since 1.24). Shots with you in them count you in with the flash, keep the screen clean and replay the take straight after.

MATCH CUT (one move done once at every place, see below) and SILHOUETTE (darkened so you stay black against the sky) count towards a trip's total, as the saved reels use them a lot. B-ROLL is six different kinds of footage that show the place, 6 to 12 seconds each, and stops by itself so you can grab several in a row; WILDLIFE (animals on the 5x, tagged wildlife) is an extra. The creative shots are extras, filed through the day with the rest: CLONE (one long take, 3 minutes until you change it, with focus, exposure and white balance locked: the frame empty for 10 seconds, then you doing something different at a new spot whenever the flash says, then out of shot and empty again, for the edit to make the clones), ORBIT, HYPERLAPSE (with a crosshair), REFLECTION (landscape, with the camera just above still water) and DANCE. WHIP (Out and In halves), STAY (walking into where you're staying, for stay tours) and TIMELAPSE are extras too. REVEAL has three versions: Auto zoom, Walk reveal and Tilt up (from the water or the ground slowly up to the view). TALK has two, both on the front camera so you can see yourself (the tripod one since 1.24): on the tripod, or walking for walk-and-talk guides. REACTION is filmed on the back camera for the best picture (1.24): held at arm's length facing you, screen away, at 1x or 0.5x and 60 fps so the moment slows down smoothly. You walk up, react, then turn the phone round to the view, all in one take, and stop it once the screen's facing you again.

DANCE plays a song in your AirPods (or on the speaker) while you dance or lip-sync. Songs come from a video, screen recording or audio file and are kept in On My iPhone › Shot Guide › Songs for every trip. The song starts during the countdown so the chosen point lands as recording starts: carry on from 10 seconds before where the last take ended, start from the top, or pick a point. Each clip logs the song and exactly where in it the clip starts (allowing for Bluetooth delay), and the song is copied into the trip folder (`SONG_<name>.m4a`) so it reaches the SSD for cutting to the beat.

Clips record in Apple Log wherever the phone allows it (up to 60 fps; slow motion stays in the normal colour), which keeps the most for grading. Everything is recorded in HEVC, which keeps files small: ProRes can't film 4K60 to the phone, so if a format can't do Log in HEVC, that shot films in the normal colour instead. The camera and review screens show the picture through a look, so it looks finished while you film: Standard (Apple Log made to look like the normal camera) or a .cube LUT added from Files with the Look button on the trips screen (kept in On My iPhone › Shot Guide › Looks). Diagonal stripes show where highlights are close to blowing out. White balance locks for each take, and takes at the same spot on the same lens (within 300 m and 15 minutes) share it, so they match. In golden hour and blue hour it locks to daylight instead, before and during every take, so warm light stays warm and blue light stays blue rather than being evened out (the camera says so under the shot's name). FOOD, HOME and STAY, often filmed inside under the room's lights, never lock to daylight: they keep the camera's own choice, locked for each take like the rest. The review screen can save a graded copy to Photos for posting from your phone; the clip itself stays in Log. `tools/make_standard_look.py` makes the Standard look, and the editor uses the same file.

After each take you can name the clip. The name box is filled in with the last name you used within 300 m, with recent names one tap away, and the name goes on the end of the file name. Still and slow shots get a take check for shake and tilt. While recording, Mark (or volume down) saves the moment's time with the clip. Shots that stop by themselves keep rolling 2 extra seconds for the edit. Movement shots film at 60 fps, dropping to 30 once the sun is down. Sound is recorded in stereo with wind noise reduction where the phone supports it. A plugged-in mic is kept on the tripod shots that talk in your AirPods; check one of those clips' sound to be sure it sounds as it should. The trip page (not the camera, so nothing distracts while you film) warns when the phone is getting too hot or the battery is low (1.24), and notifications come before golden hour, sunset and blue hour on a trip you're filming. VOICEOVER records sound only, for narration. Each place has its own facts (distance, time, cost, tips), shared by the days at that place and all saved in the trip's `facts.txt`. Each trip has Add from Photos for clips and photos from other cameras and screenshots of maps and bookings (each can be given a shot type), and shows how many minutes of 4K fit in the space left. TALK, REACTION, STAY and VOICEOVER show which mic is recording. The trips screen shows when this install stops opening, in orange for the last 2 days.

Every clip is saved already named (`Place_YYYY-MM-DD_SHOT_##_Name.mov`, where the place is the day's), filed under `Library/<continent>/<country>/<state>/<date> <place>/`, and listed in that trip's `catalog.csv` with its location, light, camera settings, tags, name, day, trip type, take check, colour profile, white balance, ISO, shutter and orientation (landscape or portrait). Since 1.19, nine more columns hold the creative coverage, and since 1.24 four more after them hold ONE TAKE's parts (a row for each part, with the same file, what it's part of, and where it starts and ends):

- the role, as a stable id, with its name and purpose;
- the scene's number and name, and whether the scene is suggested or confirmed;
- the planned length;
- your Usable verdict;
- the coverage status: confirmed, provisional, extra, retake, short or rejected.

## How it's built

There's no Mac involved. Every push to `main` runs `.github/workflows/build.yml` on a GitHub macOS runner:

1. XcodeGen turns `project.yml` into an Xcode project.
2. Xcode builds the app without signing it.
3. The workflow packs `ShotGuide.ipa` and puts it on the `builds` branch, along with the app's details and any build errors.

The newest app is always at <https://github.com/jayemarbi/shot-guide/raw/builds/ShotGuide.ipa>.

## Installing it (free Apple ID)

1. On a Windows PC, install [Sideloadly](https://sideloadly.io) plus iTunes and iCloud from Apple's website (not the Microsoft Store versions).
2. Plug in the iPhone, drag `ShotGuide.ipa` into Sideloadly, enter your Apple ID and click Start.
3. First time only: turn on Developer Mode (Settings › Privacy & Security › Developer Mode), restart, then trust the app under Settings › General › VPN & Device Management.

Apps signed with a free Apple ID stop opening after 7 days. Install the same file again over the top; trips and clips stay. Deleting the app deletes its clips, so copy trips to the PC first.

## Getting footage off the phone

Both ways first get the clips ready for editing, on the phone, once per clip: a cutout mask (`<clip>_mask.mov`, white where you are) for every shot you're in up to 2 minutes long (longer takes like ONE TAKE, CLONE and DANCE are left to the editor), and captions (`<clip>.srt`, plus the text in the catalogue) for TALK, REACTION, STAY and VOICEOVER. Keep the app open while that runs.

**With an SSD (the main way).** Plug the SSD into the iPhone. On a trip's page, tap **Export trip** › **Copy to SSD or folder**, and choose the SSD. The trip goes into `Shot Guide/<continent>/<country>/<state>/<date> <place>/` beside the trips already there, with its `catalog.csv` and `trip.json`. Clips already on the drive are skipped, so copying a trip again only adds new takes, and clips renamed since the last copy are renamed on the drive too. After a copy, **Free up space** takes the copied clips off the phone; the trip keeps its shot list, names and counts. The SSD needs to be formatted exFAT (or APFS) for the iPhone to write to it, and exFAT also works on Windows.

**Without the SSD.** **Export trip** › **Make a .zip file** packs the trip into one file with the same `Shot Guide/...` folders inside. Copy it to the PC with iTunes (phone button near the top left › **File Sharing** › Shot Guide › **Save to**), or the Apple Devices app (Files › Shot Guide), or share it by AirDrop. Unzip it in the folder that holds your `Shot Guide` folder and it slots in beside the other trips.

## Code map

| Folder | What's in it |
| --- | --- |
| `ShotGuide/Models` | The shots and their timelines, trip types and how they tune the list, trips, days and clips, tags, countries and states |
| `ShotGuide/Services` | Saving trips and the catalogue, looks and graded copies, location, sun position and light alerts, tag suggestions, masks and captions, the install's expiry |
| `ShotGuide/Camera` | Camera set-up per shot (Apple Log where possible), recording, time-lapse, the live preview through the look, the level and take check |
| `ShotGuide/Views` | Trip list, new trip, days and trip types, shot list, camera screen, review, naming and tagging |
